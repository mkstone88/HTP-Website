If you've been saving pictures of white-brick houses, you've probably noticed they don't all look the same. Some have crisp, solid coverage where every brick is one uniform color. Others have a soft, weathered look where the brick's texture and some of its original color still show through. The first is **painted brick**; the second is what most people mean when they say **limewash**.

Homeowners ask us to settle the painted-vs-limewash debate all the time, so here's the honest answer up front: **there's no right or wrong choice. It's purely aesthetic.** Both finishes, done with the right products, protect the brick and last for years. The only question that matters is which look you want to pull into your driveway and see every day.

## The two products are more alike than you'd think

We use [Romabio](https://romabio.com/masonry/) mineral products for brick work, and their two main options are closely related:

- **Romabio Masonry Flat** is a breathable mineral paint. It gives solid, uniform coverage in any tint you choose, with a flat finish. Because it bonds to the masonry instead of forming a film over it, it won't peel or flake the way ordinary paint on brick eventually does, and Romabio backs it with a 20-year product warranty when properly applied.
- **Romabio Classico Limewash** is an authentic slaked-lime wash. It goes on soft and slightly translucent, and while it's still workable we can distress it, washing some back off in places so the brick shows through. The result is the old-world, sun-faded look you see on European farmhouses (and half of Pinterest).

Both are lime/mineral-based and breathable, which matters more than any style question: brick has to vent moisture, and ordinary house paint seals it in, which is how brick gets damaged. Whatever look you pick, the product should be made for masonry. We cover that in depth on our [brick painting service page](/brick-painting-okc/).

## So how do you choose?

Here's the conversation we have at estimates:

**Choose painted brick (Masonry Flat) if** you want a clean, finished, contemporary look (crisp white, warm cream, even a dramatic dark color) and you want every wall uniform. It's also the stronger choice if your brick has repairs, replaced sections, or mismatched mortar you'd like to disappear: solid coverage hides all of it.

**Choose limewash (Classico) if** you love the brick's texture and want it to stay part of the story. Limewash reads softer and more lived-in, and no two houses come out identical. The amount of distressing is adjustable, from barely-washed hints of brick at the corners to heavy, patchy exposure, so "limewash" is really a whole range of looks, not one.

One practical note on permanence: both finishes should be treated as permanent decisions. Fresh limewash can be adjusted or even washed off within the first few days of application, but once it cures, it's staying, and removing any coating from brick after the fact is very difficult.
