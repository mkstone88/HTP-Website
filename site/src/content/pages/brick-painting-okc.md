## Painted Brick vs. Limewash: Which Look Do You Want?

Romabio's Masonry Flat and Classico Limewash are similar mineral-based, breathable products — the real difference is the look. When homeowners say "limewash," what they usually mean is they don't want solid coverage; they want some form of a distressed, weathered finish where the brick still shows through. There's no right or wrong answer — it's purely an aesthetic choice, and there's a huge variety of looks we can achieve depending on the distressing method.

- **Masonry Flat** gives solid, uniform coverage: the crisp painted-brick look, in any tint you choose.
- **Classico Limewash** gives the soft, old-world washed look, distressed to taste while it's workable.

## Why Brick Paint Has to Breathe

> “(Paint) should be porous if applied on exterior masonry, thereby permitting the wall to breathe and preventing the trapping of free moisture behind the paint film.”
> — [Brick Industry Association](https://www.gobrick.com/docs/default-source/read-research-documents/technicalnotes/6-painting-brick-masonry.pdf?sfvrsn=0), *Painting Brick Masonry*

Unpainted brick is naturally porous: moisture that gets into the wall evaporates back out. Seal it under ordinary non-porous paint and that moisture is trapped inside the masonry, where it damages the brick over time — that's how you end up with the flaking, peeling painted brick that gives this whole idea a bad name. Mineral paints like [Romabio Masonry Flat](https://romabio.com/masonry/) stay breathable, bond to the masonry rather than filming over it, won't peel or flake like traditional paint, and don't require primer on unpainted brick — which saves labor and saves you money.
