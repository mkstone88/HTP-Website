A historic home is not a bigger version of a new build. Original wood siding, ornamental trim, and decades of layered paint mean prep is most of the job: the old coating has to come off properly, the bare wood has to be primed, and details worth preserving can't be sanded into oblivion or buried under a sloppy coat. Get that wrong and the paint fails in a couple of years. Get it right and you protect the house and its character at the same time. That's the work we do in Mesta Park.

## Mesta Park Painting FAQ

<details class="faq-item"><summary>Do you have experience with historic Mesta Park homes?</summary><div><p>Yes. Mesta Park’s early-1900s homes (original wood siding, ornamental trim, and decades of layered paint) need more careful prep than a modern build. We scope each home individually and protect the character that makes these houses special.</p></div></details>
<details class="faq-item"><summary>What does it cost to paint a historic home in Mesta Park?</summary><div><p>Because every historic home is different (size, height, trim detail, and how much prep the old paint needs), Mesta Park projects are quoted individually rather than off a flat rate. Exterior repaints here generally start around $6,000 and rise with the home’s size and detail. You’ll get a firm, itemized number at your free estimate, never a surprise later.</p></div></details>
<details class="faq-item"><summary>Can you match the original or period-appropriate colors?</summary><div><p>Yes. We can match an existing color or help you choose something suited to the home's era and the character of the street. We'll put samples up so you can see them on your own siding, in your own light, before committing.</p></div></details>
<details class="faq-item"><summary>How long does a historic exterior take?</summary><div><p>Longer than a modern build, because the prep is more involved. Most run beyond the typical 2–4 day exterior, and we'll give you a realistic timeline in writing with your estimate rather than a number we can't hold to.</p></div></details>
